# The Verdant Accord — the year (Chapters 1–8 of 12)

A forest-frontier campaign with **one chapter per month** and a **legacy
outcome** at the end of every chapter. Chapters 1–8 are written (208 pages,
Jan 1 to Aug 31); Chapters 9–10 are outlined below, and 11–12 are placeholders
awaiting the next boxes. The campaign is **partial**: its catalog entry says
`chaptersWritten: 8` of `chaptersPlanned: 12`, and the end-of-content view and the
New Campaign card tell the player more is coming. Chapter 8 ends on a hook
into Chapter 9.

Its *structure* follows the user's twelve-part adventure subscription: a
caravan journey to a great city, then a run of city adventures (a flooded
district, the sewers and a sunken chapel, a riddle hunt during a festival),
each chapter with a won-or-lost legacy result that later chapters remember.
From Chapter 5 the story turns into a chase (the hero carries the Crown while
two rival powers close in), then a sea voyage, a trap-filled temple on a
volcanic island and a mountain climb. **Every name, character, place, monster and plot beat here
is original to Questender.** None of that series' story, NPCs, places, maps,
art or monsters are reused.

## The premise

**Highmarrow** is a walled city built inside the ring of the **Marrow**, the
hollow stump of a tree so old it fell before anyone kept calendars. Around it
lies the **Fernreach**: a forest-country of deep green roads, river fords and
villages that pay the woods the same respect they pay the weather.

Four hundred years ago the city's founders made the **Verdant Accord** with
the forest itself. Highmarrow may stand inside the Marrow so long as, once
every hundred years on the longest night, the **Seedcrown** (a circlet of
living seeds that never sprout) is carried back to the **Heartwood** for one
night. This is the hundredth year. Almost no one in Highmarrow believes the
old story any more.

The Seedcrown is gone from its reliquary. As the year turns, the Fernreach
starts to stir: roots cross roads overnight, beasts grow bold, rivers jam and
flood, and the oldest trees whisper *give it back*.

**Provost Ilsabet Crane** runs the **Copperleaf Exchange**, the merchant house
that owns most of the city's debts. She took the Crown. She believes the
Accord is a fairy tale, and when it lapses the Exchange will own the right to
fell the old forest for a fortune in heartwood timber. She is charming, patient
and never in the room when anything illegal happens.

## The cast

- **The hero** signs on as a guard for a caravan bound for Highmarrow.
- **Agathe Rook** is the caravan's wagon-mistress: tall, sixty, tireless,
  with a whip she never uses on animals. She is the prologue character and
  the hero's first patron. Her fortunes depend on Chapter 1's legacy.
- **Provost Ilsabet Crane** of the Copperleaf Exchange is the rival all year.
- **Ottoline Spindle** is a clockmaker who knew where the Crown went. She
  vanished before the Spring Fair and left a trail of riddles (Chapter 4).
- **The Mosswife** is the forest's messenger: a woman made of bark and lichen
  who appears at crossroads with warnings. She is never fought.

## Pacing

Same as the other classic-rules campaigns: Mon–Fri pages plus a combined
Sat/Sun page, dated against 2022 (Jan 1 was a Saturday). Most weekends are
**havens** (Rest + market, sometimes a follower to hire). Each chapter has a
mid-month set-piece and one solo boss on its last Friday. **The boss decides
the chapter's legacy** (won or lost), and the hero levels up the weekend after.

## Legacy

Each chapter records one of two marks. Later chapters offer extra
options that need a mark (shown as *Legacy: …* on the option). The first
option of every step is never gated, so no outcome can leave the hero stuck, and
a requirement only names a mark awarded on an earlier page.

From **Chapter 5** the marks are a running tally: a win is **Accord kept**, a
loss is **Wilds let in**. In each chapter's finale, earlier wins unlock options
that soften the fight (an ally holds a street, a wave is skipped, a heal or a
bonus); earlier losses unlock grim options (a debt comes due, a grudge costs
coin) that carry a price. Neither is ever the first option.
